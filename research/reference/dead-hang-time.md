# How Long a Normal Person Can Dead Hang

## Summary

There is no measured population average for the dead hang, and every published
"by age" chart is expert opinion rather than data. The one published measurement
of ordinary people is a 25-person study of *trained* recreational athletes aged
about 20: men averaged **64 s**, women **48 s**, with a standard deviation over
half the mean ([GRIP-6 2025 via deadhangs.com](https://deadhangs.com/dead-hang-time-by-age/)).
For an untrained adult, the realistic range is far lower: roughly **10–30 s**,
with trainer-reported beginner bands of 10–20 s for people over 50 and 30 s for
people under 50 ([Health](https://www.health.com/how-long-should-you-be-able-to-hold-a-dead-hang-11983865)).
Anything from 30–60 s already sits in intermediate territory; 60 s+ is advanced;
the world record is 80 min 41 s
([Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/118967-longest-duration-in-the-dead-hang-position)).

## Details

The bands do not agree with each other. "Intermediate" means 21–45 s to one
publisher and 60–90 s to another
([deadhangs.com comparison](https://deadhangs.com/dead-hang-time-by-age/)).

The coach source behind the two-row age table, Delgado-Lugo, states plainly that
there is little standardised data on dead hangs and that her numbers come from
training clients ([Health](https://www.health.com/how-long-should-you-be-able-to-hold-a-dead-hang-11983865)).
Marathon Handbook: "there aren't any official standards so we really can't be
exact" ([Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/good-hang-time/)).

### The only published measurement

Chomiuk et al., *J Funct Morphol Kinesiol* 2025;10(4):427 ("GRIP-6"): 25 healthy
recreational athletes, 11 men, 14 women, mean age 20.4, overhand shoulder-width
grip, straight arms, three trials averaged. Passive hang to failure.

| Group | n | Before 6 weeks | After 6 weeks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Men | 11 | 64.0 s (SD 35.3) | 69.4 s (SD 33.7) |
| Women | 14 | 48.4 s (SD 29.1) | 54.3 s (SD 28.7) |
| All | 25 | 55.3 s | 60.9 s |

Figures as tabulated at
[deadhangs.com](https://deadhangs.com/dead-hang-time-by-age/).

The sample is young and already athletic, so it overstates a general population.
Individual spread is roughly ±30 s at one standard deviation — a 10 s difference
between two people says little.

An older, smaller, male-only reference: Drury et al. 2004 had 32 moderately
trained men average 95.4 s on a standard bar and 107.6 s on a Tri-Bar, a
different bar geometry
([deadhangs.com](https://deadhangs.com/dead-hang-time-by-age/)).

### Stronger-person and record marks, for scale

- Peter Attia's podcast target for a "very strong" 40-year-old: **≥120 s** male,
  **≥90 s** female, minus about 10 s per decade. A target, not a norm
  ([deadhangs.com](https://deadhangs.com/dead-hang-time-by-age/)).
- World record, dead hang position, male: **80 min 41 s**, Kenta Adachi, Japan,
  18 Nov 2022 ([Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/118967-longest-duration-in-the-dead-hang-position)). The previous record was 16 min 3 s
  ([Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/asia_pacific/2023-03-21/dead-hang-guinness-world-record-9566782.html)).

### What actually limits the time

Grip endurance at a load fixed to your own body mass. Lighter people hang
longer at equal absolute grip strength, so a hang-time comparison across very
different bodyweights is misleading
([Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/good-hang-time/)). Bar
diameter, sweat, and how much you have practised anything all move the number
([Health](https://www.health.com/how-long-should-you-be-able-to-hold-a-dead-hang-11983865)).

## Gotchas

- **Hang time is not grip strength.** A dynamometer measures peak force over ~3 s;
  a hang measures submaximal force held over time. In the 2025 training study,
  hang times improved in every group while maximal grip strength improved
  inconsistently ([Outside](https://www.outsideonline.com/health/wellness/dead-hang-challenge-longevity/)).
- **Hang time is not a validated prediction of anything.** Grip strength is
  associated with mortality in large cohorts — each 5 kg lower grip, 16% higher
  all-cause mortality in the 140,000-person PURE cohort
  ([Lancet 2015](https://www.lancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(15)60676-6/fulltext));
  replicated for cardiovascular, respiratory and cancer outcomes in ~500,000 UK
  Biobank participants ([BMJ 2018](https://www.bmj.com/content/361/bmj.k1651)) —
  but no study has shown that *hang time* predicts mortality. Carrying the grip
  numbers over to the bar is an assumption
  ([Outside](https://www.outsideonline.com/health/wellness/dead-hang-challenge-longevity/)).
- **No percentiles exist.** A 60 s hang cannot truthfully be called "top 25%";
  there is no comparison dataset
  ([deadhangs.com](https://deadhangs.com/dead-hang-time-by-age/)).
- **Test conditions must be held constant** — same bar, same grip width, chalk or
  none, fixed regripping rule — or the series is noise
  ([Outside](https://www.outsideonline.com/health/wellness/dead-hang-challenge-longevity/)).
- Rock-climbing hangboard research is often cited for hang protocols but uses
  added load and different grip types; treat its numbers separately from a
  bodyweight dead hang ([PMC6458579](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6458579/)).

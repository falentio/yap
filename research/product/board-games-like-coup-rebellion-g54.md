# Board Games Like Coup: Rebellion G54 — Ranked Recommendations

## TL;DR

- The anchor game is **Coup: Rebellion G54** — a 2–6 player, ~15–30 minute claim-and-challenge bluffing card game that ships a variable deck of **25 characters**, of which five are chosen each game ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_(card_game)), [Space-Biff!](https://spacebiff.com/2015/11/03/coup-g54/)).
- **Same 15-minute filler slot:** buy **Skull** first (the purest read-the-person bluff), then **Cockroach Poker** (the mandatory-lie cousin), then **Coup (original)** for the tightest version of the same engine ([Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/), [Nordic Pirates](https://nordicpirates.com/blog/best-bluffing-board-games/)).
- **Bigger table, longer night:** **The Resistance: Avalon** is G54's own dystopian universe at 5–10 players, and **One Night Ultimate Werewolf** is the 10-minute version of the same hidden-role tension ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Resistance_(game)), [Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/)).
- **The genre's ceiling is Blood on the Clocktower** — widely called the best social-deduction game made — but it is a 30–120 min, 5–20 player, storyteller-run commitment, not a filler ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_on_the_Clocktower), [Eneba](https://www.eneba.com/hub/collectibles/best-social-deduction-games/)).
- The single most important filter: **do you want the *claim-and-challenge* snap decision back (Skull, Cockroach Poker, Coup), or the *hidden-team* argument that runs 30 minutes (Avalon, Secret Hitler, Clocktower)?** Those are different genres that recommendation lists routinely blur ([Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/)).

## Context

Question: *"I played Coup: Rebellion G54 — what should I play next, ranked?"* This file ranks candidate board/card games by how well they deliver what G54 delivers, and by table size and session length, so the pick takes minutes instead of a rabbit hole. Researched 2026-10-10 via TinyFish. Player counts, playtimes, and design details are cited; where a number is only available from a secondary source it is attributed rather than stated flatly.

**So the profile to match:** small table, light rules, ≤30 minutes, high table-talk, and bluffing that is *declared out loud and punished immediately* rather than quietly hidden until the end.

## Ranking method

Two axes decide almost every pick ([Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/)):

1. **Bluff family** — *claim-and-challenge* (you assert something, someone calls it, resolution is instant: Skull, Cockroach Poker, Coup) vs *hidden-role* (you are secretly on a team, the argument accumulates: Avalon, Secret Hitler, Clocktower).
2. **Table size × session length** — a filler for 4, or a 30-minute game for 8.

A third, softer axis is **rules weight** (how long to teach). G54's whole appeal is that you can teach it while shuffling, so heavy teach-time is treated as a cost.

## Ranked shortlist

### Filler tier — the same 15-minute slot as G54

Compared across the columns; dimensions down the rows.

| Dimension | Skull | Cockroach Poker | Coup (original) | Coup: Reformation | Mascarade |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Players | 3–6 | 2–6 | 2–6 | 2–10 | 2–13 |
| Playtime | 15–45 min | ~15–20 min | ~15 min | ~15–30 min | 30+ min |
| Bluff family | Claim-and-challenge (bidding) | Claim-and-challenge (mandatory lie) | Claim-and-challenge | Claim-and-challenge | Hidden-role + memory |
| Rules weight | Lowest | Very low | Very low | Low | Low–medium |
| Why a G54 player wants it | Betting entirely on reading a person, not odds | Every single hand-off is a lie — deeper, meaner | The tightest version of the exact engine you already like | Same engine, now 10 players, factions + Inquisitor | Bluff *and* remember who holds what |
| Catch | Wants 4+; thin at 3 | Ugly art is intentional; gangs up on the leader | You may find it a step *down* from G54 variety | Needs the base game; faction layer adds teach time | Getting lost is part of it — some hate that |
| Source | [Nordic Pirates](https://nordicpirates.com/blog/best-bluffing-board-games/), [Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/) | [Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/) | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_(card_game)) | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_(card_game)) | [Jesta ThaRogue](https://www.jestatharogue.com/game-reviews/review-mascarade/) |

1. **Skull** — the top pick if what you love about G54 is the staring contest. Four discs, three roses and one skull, bid on how many you can flip without hitting a skull; the bluff is a single decision and the table goes silent before a big flip. Hexagamers ranks it **#1** of "card games like Coup" ([Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/)); Nordic Pirates calls it "the purest bluffing game ever made" ([Nordic Pirates](https://nordicpirates.com/blog/best-bluffing-board-games/)).
2. **Cockroach Poker** — the deeper cut and the "true mechanical cousin." The lie is mandatory and constant (every card handoff), so you end up bluffing about a bluff about a bluff; it plays 2–6 and rewards targeting the player who is nearly dead ([Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/)).
3. **Coup (original)** — the canonical five roles, 2–6 players, ~15 min, tighter than G54 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_(card_game))). Buy it only if you want the *simpler* version; if you already own G54, skip to Skull/Cockroach Poker for variety.
4. **Coup: Reformation** — the base game plus Loyalist/Reformist factions and an Inquisitor role, and it plays up to **10 players** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup_(card_game))). This is the cheapest way to get the exact G54 engine to a bigger table.
5. **Mascarade** — Bruno Faidutti's bluff-plus-memory game for **2–13 players, 30+ min**; roles are swapped face-down so even *you* may not know what you are, which is the accidental-lying thrill G54 players tend to enjoy ([Jesta ThaRogue](https://www.jestatharogue.com/game-reviews/review-mascarade/)).

### Night tier — bigger table, longer session

| Dimension | The Resistance: Avalon | One Night Ultimate Werewolf | Secret Hitler | Deception: Murder in Hong Kong | Blood on the Clocktower |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Players | 5–10 | 3–10 | 5–10 | 4–12 | 5–20 |
| Playtime | 15–30 min | ~10 min | 30–60 min | ~30 min | 30–120 min |
| Bluff family | Hidden-team | Hidden-role | Hidden-team (political) | Deduction + one killer | Hidden-team, storyteller-run |
| Rules weight | Low | Low (needs free app) | Medium | Low–medium | High (a gamemaster) |
| Why a G54 player wants it | G54's own universe; lies accumulate over rounds | The 10-minute, no-elimination version of Werewolf | Team argument with real vote pressure | A puzzle with a hidden traitor — different muscle | Best-in-class; nobody is ever eliminated |
| Catch | Needs 5 players minimum | App narration is mandatory | Heavier teach; sensitive theme | Deduction-heavy, lighter on bluffing | Full-evening commitment + a Storyteller |
| Source | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Resistance_(game)) | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Werewolf), [Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/) | [Eneba](https://www.eneba.com/hub/collectibles/best-social-deduction-games/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Hitler) | [What's Eric Playing](https://whatsericplaying.com/2017/06/12/deception-murder-in-hong-kong/) | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_on_the_Clocktower) |

6. **The Resistance: Avalon** — shares G54's dystopian universe and is "where Coup groups tend to graduate." Hidden teams, mission voting, and lies that get re-examined rounds later; 5–10 players, 15–30 min. It also fixes Coup's "dies if nobody bluffs" weakness, because the evil team has no honest option ([Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Resistance_(game))).
7. **One Night Ultimate Werewolf** — compresses Werewolf into one night and one vote so nobody sits out after elimination; 3–10 players, ~10 min, narrated by a free app. The counter-claim ("no, *I am* the Seer") is the exact G54 moment, and role-swapping means you can lie by accident ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Werewolf), [Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/)).
8. **Secret Hitler** — 5–10 players, 30–60 min, re-engineers Avalon's gameplay with Werewolf influence; the deeper, louder team-argument game, though with a heavier teach and a deliberately provocative theme ([Eneba](https://www.eneba.com/hub/collectibles/best-social-deduction-games/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Hitler)).
9. **Deception: Murder in Hong Kong** — 4–12 players, ~30 min; a forensic Scientist gives clues while a hidden Murderer hides among investigators. It blends social deduction with a genuine logic puzzle, so it is *less* pure bluffing than G54 but scales well ([What's Eric Playing](https://whatsericplaying.com/2017/06/12/deception-murder-in-hong-kong/)).
10. **Blood on the Clocktower** — 5–20 players, 30–120 min, run by a Storyteller. Named the "all-round best social deduction game" by Wargamer and #1 by Eneba; it fixes the two classic problems (early elimination and no information) by keeping dead players talking and voting. The cost is time, a gamemaster, and (if you buy the physical box) money ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_on_the_Clocktower), [Eneba](https://www.eneba.com/hub/collectibles/best-social-deduction-games/)).
11. **Ultimate Werewolf** — the classic, 5–75 players, 15–45 min; pure mayhem for huge rooms, but it needs a moderator and eliminates players early ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Werewolf), [Eneba](https://www.eneba.com/hub/collectibles/best-social-deduction-games/)).
12. **Feed the Kraken** — a three-faction role-deduction game that goes up to **11 players**; consistently named in BGG "best social deduction" discussions, but the playtime was **not confirmed** in this pass ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/271601/feed-the-kraken)) [unverified].

### Also consider (adjacent, one line each)

- **Sheriff of Nottingham** — 3–5 players, 45–60 min; Coup's snap challenge stretched into a bribery negotiation ([Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/)).
- **Spyfall** — 3–8 players, 6–15 min; one player does not know the location and must bluff knowledge ([Nordic Pirates](https://nordicpirates.com/blog/best-bluffing-board-games/)).
- **Love Letter** — 2–6 players, 20 min; heavy deduction, light bluffing — the *opposite* mix to G54, but the same "another quick one" slot ([Z-Man Games](https://www.zmangames.com/game/love-letter/), [Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/)).
- **Citadels** — 2–8 players, 30–60 min; the role-drafting game Coup's characters descend from; best around 5–6 ([Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/)).
- **Saboteur** — 3–10 players, ~30 min; hidden traitor, no challenge mechanic, very forgiving on player count ([Hexagamers](https://hexagamers.com/card-games-like-coup/)).
- **Chameleon** — 3–8 players, 15 min; one player fakes knowledge of a secret word; award-winning quick party deduction ([Eneba](https://www.eneba.com/hub/collectibles/best-social-deduction-games/)).
- **Lying Pirates** — 2–6 players, 40–60 min; Liar's Dice bluffing plus a race; note this is the publisher's own list ([Nordic Pirates](https://nordicpirates.com/blog/best-bluffing-board-games/)).

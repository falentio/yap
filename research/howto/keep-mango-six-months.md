# Keep Mango for Six Months — What Actually Lasts, by Method

## Goal

Take ripe mangoes and still be eating them six months later, knowing which method you are
buying into and what each one costs you in texture, flavor, and effort. The short answer:
**six months is reachable, but never as a fresh mango.** Fresh mango tops out around three
weeks even in commercial controlled-atmosphere storage; the six-month horizon belongs to
freezing (plainest), drying (shelf-stable), and canning (only as an acidified or under-ripe
product).

## Prerequisites

| You need | For |
|----------|-----|
| Freezer at 0 °F / −18 °C, reliable | freezing, purée |
| Freezer bags or rigid containers, moisture-vapor resistant | freezing |
| Dehydrator holding 140 °F / 60 °C, or a dry sunny climate | drying |
| Boiling-water canner, jars, two-piece lids | canning |
| Firm-ripe, blemish-free fruit | every method — preservation does not improve fruit |

Ripe mangoes are climacteric: they keep ripening off the tree, so "fresh" is a race you can
slow, never stop. Postharvest losses across the whole supply chain reach up to 50% depending
on cultivar and handling ([Frontiers, 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2021.799431/full)).

## Steps

### 1. Pick the method against the 6-month target

Compared across the columns; dimensions down the rows.

| Dimension | Fresh, fridge | Cold + controlled atmosphere | Freeze | Dry / dehydrate | Boil-water can |
|-----------|---------------|------------------------------|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| Horizon at 6 months | no — spoils in days | no — weeks, not months | yes, best quality | yes, shelf-stable | yes, only acidified |
| Realistic maximum | ~5 days ripe in a fridge; ~20 days at 13 °C | ~3–6 weeks with CA gas mix | 8–12 months at 0 °F | 6 months proven, 33–41 months predicted at safe water activity | 1 year sealed |
| What you keep | fruit as-is | ripening fruit | texture partly, flavor mostly | chewy slices, leather | syrup-packed, cooked |
| What you lose | everything, eventually | a little aroma at high CO2 | firmness — hardness drops measurably over 6 months | water, some vitamin C | fresh texture entirely |
| Equipment | fridge | CA room + gas control (commercial) | freezer | dehydrator | canner |
| Effort | none | industrial | low | medium | high |
| Home-doable | — | no | yes | yes | only green mango or tested acidified recipe |

Anything claiming a fresh mango in a fridge at six months is selling a different product.

### 2. Freeze it — the default six-month route

Firm-ripe fruit, washed, peeled, sliced. Then pick a pack
([NCHFP](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/freeze/fruits/freezing-mangos)):

- **Syrup pack** — cover slices with 30% syrup, leave headspace, seal, freeze.
- **Unsweetened tray pack** — freeze slices individually on a flat pan, then bag them. Best
  when you want loose pieces for smoothies.
- **Purée** — sieve or blend, pack with or without sugar.

Push all the air out, label with date, hold at 0 °F or below. NCHFP rates fruits and
vegetables at **8–12 months at 0 °F**, and notes that past that window the food stays safe and
just loses quality ([NCHFP](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/freeze/freeze-general-information/how-long-can-i-store-frozen-foods)).
Six months sits comfortably inside the window, not at its edge.

Expect the texture to move: frozen storage at −18 °C for 6 months produced a significant
decrease in mango hardness
([study indexed on Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35159639)). Freeze cubes for
smoothies, purée, and sauces; a thawed slice eaten out of hand is the one use that disappoints.

Industrial purée takes the same idea further: heat to 90 °C for 1 min, cool to 35 °C, fill
lined tins, freeze at −23.5 °C
([FAO, Fruit and vegetable processing](https://www.fao.org/4/v5030e/v5030e0p.htm)).

### 3. Dry it — for a pantry instead of a freezer

Slice ¼ inch (6 mm) thick and dry at 140 °F / 60 °C for **10–12 hours**, until pliable; or
blend under-ripe fruit into a leather and dry ¼ inch thick for about 10 hours
([Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/preserving-tropical-fruits)).

Get the water out far enough or the six months is fiction. Safe storage means roughly **15%
moisture or less, about 0.6 water activity**
([Wiley, 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jfpp.15041)). A blanched,
treated dried-slice trial stored the product for **6 months** and reported the nutritional
outcome over that period
([Polish Journal of Food and Nutrition Sciences, 2018](https://journal.pan.olsztyn.pl/Effect-of-Blanching-Techniques-and-Treatments-on-Nutritional-Quality-of-Dried-Mango,98509,0,2.html)).
Better-dried solar samples were modelled at 33–41 months of predicted shelf life
([Mongi, 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666154323000297)) — the
model, not the jar on your shelf, is what is that optimistic.

Store airtight, cool, dark. Vitamin C is the first thing to fade; count drying as trading
freshness for shelf stability, not for nutrition parity.

### 4. Can it — only where there is a tested recipe

This is the step people get wrong. Oregon State Extension is explicit: as mangoes ripen they
become **less acidic**, and there are **no safe tested guidelines for canning ripe mangoes or
mango sauce**; a mango chutney or salsa is safe only because the tested recipe adds lemon
juice or vinegar ([OSU Extension SP 50-1006](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/food/preservation/preserving-fruits-mangoes)).

What is tested:

- **Green mangoes**, hot pack in light or medium syrup, ½ inch headspace, boiling-water
  canner: **15 min pints, 20 min quarts** below 1,000 ft; add 5 min per 1,000–3,000 ft
  ([NCHFP](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/can/canning-fruits-and-fruit-products/mangoes-green),
  [OSU](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/food/preservation/preserving-fruits-mangoes)).
- **Acidified products** — chutney, salsa, mango sauce from a tested recipe.

For a commercial-scale alternative, FAO's route is acidify pulp to **pH 3.5**, pasteurize at
90 °C, hot-fill into sterilized HDPE containers
([FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/v5030e/v5030e0p.htm)). That is a process, not a kitchen step.

### 5. Know why "keep it fresh longer" caps out in weeks

Cold storage slows ripening: 13 °C is the industry standard and buys roughly **20 days**;
13 °C plus an oxalic-acid treatment reached 49 days in one study
([Foods, 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/12/1/173)). Go colder and you trade spoilage for
injury: below **10 °C** mature-green mangoes show abnormal ripening, peel discoloration, skin
pitting, carotenoid and aroma loss, and worse fungal susceptibility — and it gets worse when
the fruit returns to ambient
([Frontiers, 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2021.799431/full)).

Controlled atmosphere is the strongest cold-storage lever: **2% O₂ + 6% CO₂ at 13 °C** is
described as potent for extending shelf life while holding quality, and 2% O₂ + 2% CO₂
preserved ripe-mango aroma compounds better
([Frontiers, 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2021.799431/full)).
Reviews of the field put CA work in the same weeks-to-a-couple-months band, with oxygen
tolerance differing by cultivar
([Ntsoane et al., 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S030442381930041X)).
No home setup reproduces it, and none of it reaches six months.

## Verification

| Claim | Check before you commit |
|-------|-------------------------|
| Freezer holds 0 °F / −18 °C | a fridge/freezer thermometer, read after a full day |
| Slices are dry enough | a dried slice is pliable, not brittle, and no moisture beads when squeezed |
| Jars sealed | lid concave, rings off, tapping gives a ringing tone ([NCHFP](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/can/general-information/storing-home-canned-foods/)) |
| Recipe is tested | it comes from NCHFP, USDA, or a university extension — not a blog |

The real verification is at month six: thaw a bag and taste it. Frozen mango that spent six
months at a stable 0 °F tastes close to fresh in a smoothie; the same bag through a freezer
thaw cycle tastes flat and watery.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Frozen slices mushy on thaw | ice crystals from slow freezing; tissue breakdown | freeze on a tray first, then bag; use for purée |
| Freezer burn, dry white patches | air in the package | press air out, use freezer-grade bags, double-bag |
| Dried slices mold in the jar | water activity too high | dry longer, condition the batch, store airtight with a desiccant |
| Canned ripe mango unsafe or fermenting | no tested process exists for low-acid ripe mango | only can green mango or tested acidified recipes |
| Fresh mango bitter or resinous near skin | sap burn, handling damage | peel deeper, discard damaged tissue |
| Skin irritation after handling green mango | Anacardiaceae family, same as poison ivy | gloves, wash hands and surfaces ([PSU](https://extension.psu.edu/preserving-tropical-fruits)) |

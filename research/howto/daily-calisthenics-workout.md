# Daily Calisthenics Workout — 7-Day Block

Companion to [Calisthenics from Zero — Beginner Progression Guide](./calisthenics-beginner-progression.md),
which holds the reasoning, the source citations, and the full progression ladders. This file
is the thing you actually do on Monday.

## Goal

A **seven-day cycle you can run every week**, where three days are strength training, three
are active recovery, and one is complete rest. Every training day paired push with pull —
the imbalance fix that matters most coming from a pull-up-only background.

## Prerequisites

Read this before session one. It is the whole difference between this working and not.

| Need | Why | If you don't have it |
|---|---|---|
| Bar you can hang from | Vertical pull ladder | Banded doorway bar, park bars; everything else still runs |
| Waist-height bar **or** sturdy table | Inverted rows | Non-negotiable — rows are the pattern you're missing most |
| Two sturdy chairs / counter corner | Dips, support holds | Skip the dip rung; pike push-ups cover vertical press |
| Floor | Everything else | — |

**Choose your starting rung before day one.** Test these once, in this order, and write the
numbers down. Every session below says "your rung" — this is how you find out what that is.

| Test | Measure | Your entry rung |
|---|---|---|
| Dead hang, shoulders engaged | seconds held | <10 s → stay here; 10–30 s → scapular pulls; 60 s+ → negatives |
| Scapular pull-up | reps, elbows straight | can't → keep drilling; 5–10 → add inverted rows |
| Inverted row, feet on floor | max clean reps | 0–3 → this is your main pull; 10–15 → elevate feet |
| Incline push-up | max clean reps, highest surface | <5 → go to wall push-ups |
| Bodyweight squat | max clean reps, hips below knees | <8 → sit-to-stand from a chair |

## Steps

### The rule that makes a daily cycle survivable

**Strength work runs 3 days a week with at least one rest day between sessions. The other
four days are not training — they are recovery.**

The Recommended Routine answers "can I do this every day?" with a flat **no**: you get
stronger and bigger when you rest ([RR](https://www.reddit.com/r/bodyweightfitness/wiki/kb/recommended_routine/)).
So this plan gives you **a session every day**, but only Monday, Wednesday and Friday load
the tissue. Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday are joint prep, mobility, and walking.

The reason this matters more for you than for a heavier trainee: at 46–52 kg you have thin
energy and glycogen reserves. Daily loading without recovery shows up as stalled rep counts
within two weeks.

### The weekly cycle at a glance

| Day | Session | Duration | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | **A — Full body strength** | 40–45 min | 3 patterns, 3 × 5–8 |
| Tue | R — Recovery & joint prep | 10–15 min | None |
| **Wed** | **B — Full body strength** | 40–45 min | 3 patterns, 3 × 5–8 |
| Thu | R — Recovery & joint prep | 10–15 min | None |
| **Fri** | **C — Full body + core** | 40–50 min | 3 patterns + core triplet |
| Sat | R — Mobility + walk | 20–30 min | None |
| Sun | Off | — | None |

Every strength day trains **push paired with pull**. That is the structural fix for a
pull-up-only history: pushing and pulling oppose each other, so training them together keeps
the shoulder balanced.

### Day A — Full body strength (Monday)

Warm-up first. Every strength day starts with the same 5-minute block.

**Warm-up (5 min)**

| Reps | Exercise |
|---|---|
| 5–10 | Shoulder dislocates — band, broomstick, or a t-shirt held taut |
| 5–10 | Squat sky reaches |
| 10 each | Wrist prep — palms forward, palms back, fingers forward, fingers backward |
| 30 s | Deadbugs, slow |

**Strength — two pairs, alternating, 90 s rest between each set**

| Order | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Tempo | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | Vertical pull — your rung (hang / scapular pull / row / banded / negative / pull-up) | 3 × 5–8 | 10X0 | Stop 1 rep short of failure |
| 1b | Squat — your rung (sit-to-stand / bodyweight / box squat) | 3 × 5–8 | 10X0 | Hips below knees, heels down |
| 2a | Horizontal pull — inverted row, feet on floor | 3 × 5–8 | 10X0 | Chest to bar, elbows in |
| 2b | Horizontal push — incline push-up on a counter | 3 × 5–8 | 10X0 | Elbows ~45° from torso |

**How to run the pairs:** set of 1a → rest 90 s → set of 1b → rest 90 s → repeat for three
rounds. Then the same for 2a/2b. This gives each muscle group ≥3 minutes between its own
sets, which is where ATP resynthesis is near-complete
([RR](https://www.reddit.com/r/bodyweightfitness/wiki/kb/recommended_routine/)).

**Tempo 10X0** — 1 second down, no bottom pause, drive up fast, no top pause. If the bar
moves slowly that's fine; the *intent* to accelerate is the stimulus.

### Day R — Recovery & joint prep (Tuesday, Thursday)

Ten to fifteen minutes. This is not a workout and should not leave you tired.

| Reps / time | Exercise | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 10 each | Wrist prep — palms forward, palms back, fingers forward, fingers backward | Wrists are the #1 beginner complaint site |
| 10 | Shoulder dislocates | Prepare the shoulder for the next strength day |
| 30 s each side | Deadbug | Anti-extension, low cost |
| 30 s | Deep squat hold, holding a doorframe for balance | Hip and ankle range |
| 10 | Cat-cow | Spine |
| 30 s | Dead hang from the bar, easy grip | Grip endurance without loading |
| 10 min | Walk | Appetite — which you specifically need |

The injury list is explicit about why this day exists: most calisthenics overuse problems are
**local** — elbows, shoulders, wrists — so prep those tissues rather than raising a light
sweat ([Berg Movement](https://www.bergmovement.com/calisthenics-blog/calisthenics-tier-list)).
Because you are underweight, the walk is not optional cardio — the NHS names exercise as an
appetite driver ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-weight/managing-your-weight/healthy-ways-to-gain-weight/)).

### Day B — Full body strength (Wednesday)

Same warm-up, different pairings. Vertical press and hinge enter the plan.

| Order | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Tempo | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | Horizontal push — incline push-up, one surface lower than Monday | 3 × 5–8 | 10X0 | Progress by lowering the surface |
| 1b | Hinge — glute bridge → single-leg glute bridge | 3 × 5–8 | 10X0 | Squeeze glutes at the top, do not arch |
| 2a | Vertical pull — your rung, same rung as Monday | 3 × 5–8 | 10X0 | Second exposure, same rung |
| 2b | Vertical push — pike push-up, hips high | 3 × 5–8 | 10X0 | Head between hands, not in front |

Pike push-ups are the entry rung for overhead pressing and they are deliberately late in the
plan: overhead work is the pattern most likely to irritate the shoulder, so it arrives with a
week of joint prep already behind it.

### Day C — Full body + core (Friday)

Warm-up, then one pair, then the core triplet. This is the longest session and the one that
closes the week.

| Order | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Tempo | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | Vertical pull — your rung | 3 × 5–8 | 10X0 | |
| 1b | Squat — your rung | 3 × 5–8 | 10X0 | |
| 2a | Anti-extension — deadbug or hollow body hold | 3 × 8–12 | control | Lower back stays flat to the floor |
| 2b | Anti-rotation — bird-dog or side plank | 3 × 8–12 | control | No hip rotation — that's the point |
| 2c | Extension — reverse crunch or superman hold | 3 × 8–12 | control | Slow, no momentum |

Core is a **triplet**: 2a → rest 60 s → 2b → rest 60 s → 2c → rest 60 s, three rounds.

### Day R — Mobility + walk (Saturday)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 20–30 min | Walk, ideally outdoors |
| 2 min | Deep squat hold, accumulated |
| 2 min | Shoulder dislocates, slow and easy |
| 1 min | Dead hang, accumulated — this is your pull-up insurance |

### How you progress

One rule, applied to every exercise, every session:

> **Do 3 × 5. Each session, add one rep per set. At 3 × 8, move up one rung and reset to 3 × 5.**

That is the Recommended Routine's own progression rule
([RR](https://www.reddit.com/r/bodyweightfitness/wiki/kb/recommended_routine/)). It has one
property worth knowing: it is unfalsifiable-proof. You never guess whether you earned the
next rung — the rep count tells you.

Exceptions:

- **Isometrics** (hangs, support holds): a "set" is a hold. Advance at 30 s across all 3 sets.
- **Core:** runs 3 × 8–12, not 5–8.
- **A rung that has no easier version left:** widen the range instead — go to 3 × 12–15
  before advancing. This is legal and useful at your bodyweight.

### Writing it down

Keep a one-page log. Four columns are enough:

```text
Date | Exercise | Sets × Reps achieved | Rung or surface
---
Feb 16 | negative pull-up | 5-5-5 | 8 s descent
Feb 16 | incline push-up | 5-5-5 | countertop
Feb 16 | inverted row | 6-5-5 | feet on floor
```

The log is the whole program. Without it you cannot tell a plateau from a bad night's sleep,
and the RR's own plateau rule — if you fail to progress for two weeks, cut volume 30% for one
week — is unusable without the record.

### Eating around the sessions (non-optional)

At 46–52 kg the training stimulus is not your bottleneck; **the surplus is**. Target
**+300–500 kcal/day** ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-weight/managing-your-weight/healthy-ways-to-gain-weight/)).

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 60–90 min before strength day | A real meal — rice, potato or bread plus protein |
| Between meals, every day | Whole milk or a milkshake; handful of nuts; Greek yoghurt with honey |
| On the two meals you already eat | Add cheese, an extra egg, or a spoon of peanut butter |
| Spread across 3–4 meals | 20–30 g protein each — even distribution beats one big hit ([PMC6566799](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6566799/)) |

Your protein target: **~74–83 g/day** at 1.6 g/kg. Roughly four eggs plus a chicken breast
clears it.

## Verification

Re-test on day one of week five. Write the numbers next to your day-one numbers.

| Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| Dead hang | ≥30 s, shoulders engaged |
| Scapular pull-up | 3 × 10–12 with a 2 s pause |
| Inverted row | 3 × 15, feet elevated |
| Negative pull-up | 5 × 3 with a 10 s+ descent |
| Incline push-up | 3 × 8 at counter height, then one surface lower |
| Bodyweight squat | 3 × 8, hips clearly below knees |
| Bodyweight, 4-week average | Up 1–2 kg |
| Sessions completed | ≥10 of 12 strength sessions |

If you clear all of those, the beginner block has done its job and a split is the next step
([solostreetworkout](https://www.solostreetworkout.com/en/beginner-calisthenics-workouts/)).

**Expected timeline.** Ability progress in 2–3 workouts. First pull-up in **8–12 weeks** at
this frequency ([odin.fitness](https://odin.fitness/blog/complete-guide-calisthenics-first-pull-up)).
Physique you notice at 4–6 weeks, others at 12+.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Reps collapse 8 → 5 → 5 | Rest too short, or first set too close to failure | Rest up to 3 min; stop 1 rep short |
| Elbow aches, inner side | Too much pulling volume, one grip | Cap pull volume; vary overhand/underhand/neutral |
| Wrist pain in push-ups | Wrist hyperextension | Do push-ups on parallettes, blocks, or fists |
| Fingers tingle after hangs | Over-gripping, or cranking the wrist | Relax the grip; check alignment |
| Shoulder pinches on pike push-ups | Overhead work ramped too fast | Regress to pike push-ups with hips lower; hold the rung a week longer |
| Tired on Tuesday/Thursday | Recovery days that became workouts | Cut them to joint prep and walking only |
| Weight flat for 3+ weeks | Surplus isn't real | Log 3 days of intake against the +300–500 target |
| No appetite after training | Normal at low bodyweight | Liquid calories between meals well before the session |
| Bored by week three | Only one rung per pattern | Nothing is wrong. This is what a strength block is. |

**Stop and see a doctor** if you get sharp pain rather than dull ache, or if pain is worse the
next day. The injury guidance is explicit: keep aches at 2–3 out of 10, and do not immobilise
— modify and regress instead ([Berg Movement](https://www.bergmovement.com/calisthenics-blog/calisthenics-tier-list)).

# Board Games Like Citadels - Ranked by Mechanical Fit

Reference game: **Citadels** (Bruno Faidutti, 2000; revised edition 2016; *Dark City* expansion 2004).
Core loop: each round every player secretly takes one numbered character (1-9), powers resolve in
number order, then you spend gold to build districts. First to 8 districts ends the game (7 with 2-3
players); score = sum of district costs + 3 for all five colours + 4 for finishing first + 2 for the
others who finished.

---

## 1. The Citadels "fingerprint" (what a clone has to match)

| # | Axis | Weight | What it means |
|---|------|--------|----------------|
| A | Secret per-round role draft, numbered resolution order, unique powers | 35% | The 1-9 characters. This is the soul of the game. |
| B | Tableau / city building from a costed pool of cards | 25% | Build 8 districts; districts have colours and costs. |
| C | Bluffing and hidden information ("who took the Assassin?") | 15% | Reading opponents, feints, King/Thief mind games. |
| D | Light-medium weight, 2-8 players, 30-60 min, gateway-plus | 15% | Plays with family and with gamers. |
| E | Set-collection scoring (five colours, finish bonus) | 10% | End-game scoring tension. |

A = role engine, B = build engine, C = table talk, D/E = shape of the session.

## 3. Top matches in detail

### 1. Citadels 2016 edition / Dark City expansion - Fit 10.0 (the baseline)
Not a similar game, the game itself. Buy this if you liked the idea: the 2016 revision folds in the
Dark City characters (including the **9th role, the Artist**) and rebalances the Assassin/Warlord.
Dark City alone is the single best purchase for a Citadels owner.

### 2. Libertalia / Libertalia: Winds of Galecrest - Fit 8.8
**Why it matches:** the purest "Citadels without the city" experience. Every player holds an
identical hand of numbered crew cards, all players reveal one card per day, and powers resolve in
rank order - so you are constantly guessing what rank your opponents will commit. Same knife-edge
mind games, same "my character got assassinated" moments, pirate theme.
**Where it differs:** no district building - you instead plunder a shared loot pool and build a
treasure tableau. Winds of Galecrest (Stonemaier, 2022) adds day/night effects, a reputation track
and a much better 2-player game.
**Best for:** groups that love Citadels for the role draft, not the board.

### 3. Glory to Rome / Uchronia - Fit 8.6
**Why it matches:** role cards (Architect, Legionary, Merchant, Senator, Laborer, Patron) plus
literal city building into your own tableau. You lead a role and everyone else may follow it - the
Citadels "everyone benefits from the role I chose" tension, turned into a follow-the-leader engine.
**Where it differs:** no secret simultaneous selection and no elimination/bluff layer; it is a
heavier, combo-heavy tableau engine. Glory to Rome is out of print and pricey - **Uchronia** (2012)
is the same designer's re-implementation and much cheaper.
**Best for:** gamers who want Citadels' roles + districts with real strategic depth.

### 4. San Juan - Fit 8.4
**Why it matches:** each round every player picks a role (Prospector, Builder, Producer, Trader,
Councillor) and all players may perform it; you build a tableau of production buildings. It is
literally "role selection + building your city", and it plays in 30-40 minutes like Citadels.
**Where it differs:** roles are chosen openly, so there is no bluffing or hidden information. The
role set is smaller and it is a bit more thinky.
**Best for:** the "build 8 districts" half of Citadels, played 2-4.

### 5. Race for the Galaxy - Fit 8.2
**Why it matches:** the same phase/role-selection engine as San Juan but everyone secretly picks a
phase simultaneously - closer to Citadels' simultaneity - then all chosen phases fire. Tableau of
worlds and developments.
**Where it differs:** zero player interaction aggression and a notoriously steep iconography curve.
**Best for:** 2-player duels and engine lovers.

### 6. Puerto Rico - Fit 8.0
**Why it matches:** the ancestor of the whole role-selection genre. The Governor picks a role and
others may follow at a cost; you build city buildings on your player board.
**Where it differs:** no hidden information, longer (90-120 min), and 3-5 players only.
**Best for:** groups that want the deep, non-bluffing version of the Citadels role engine.

### 7. Mission: Red Planet - Fit 7.9
**Why it matches:** also by Bruno Faidutti. Each round you secretly choose one of nine numbered
roles, which resolve strictly in number order and hand out actions and astronauts.
**Where it differs:** the goal is area control on Mars, not building districts; it is a step heavier.
**Best for:** Citadels fans who want the role engine with a board.

### 8. Witch's Brew - Fit 7.6
**Why it matches:** secretly choose one numbered role card per round, resolve in ascending order,
then gather ingredients. Extremely close to the Citadels turn structure.
**Where it differs:** resource conversion instead of districts; older and harder to find.

### 9. Broom Service - Fit 7.4
**Why it matches:** you play a sequence of role cards, but each turn you declare "brave" or
"cowardly" - brave does more but only if nobody else was brave. That is Citadels-style double-guessing
distilled.
**Where it differs:** no secret draft and no district tableau.

### 10. Love Letter - Fit 7.2
**Why it matches:** eight numbered roles (1 Guard to 8 Princess), one card per round, hidden hands,
elimination. It is Citadels' role ladder compressed to 16 cards and 15 minutes.
**Where it differs:** no building, no economy, very little replay depth at high skill.

## 4. Quick picks by which part of Citadels you loved

- **The secret numbered role draft:** Libertalia / Winds of Galecrest, Mission: Red Planet,
  Witch's Brew, Broom Service, Meuterer.
- **The district/city building:** Glory to Rome or Uchronia, San Juan, Alhambra, Isle of Skye,
  Suburbia, Castles of Mad King Ludwig, Machi Koro.
- **The bluffing and mind games:** Coup, Mascarade, Love Letter, Bang!, The Resistance: Avalon,
  Shadow Hunters, Spyfall.
- **The role engine with real depth:** Puerto Rico, Race for the Galaxy, San Juan, Glass Road,
  Roll for the Galaxy.
- **Best at exactly 2 players:** Citadels 2016, Libertalia: Winds of Galecrest, San Juan,
  Race for the Galaxy, Glory to Rome, 7 Wonders Duel (tableau only, no roles).
- **Best for a big table (6-8+):** Citadels 2016 (2-8), Mascarade (to 13), Bang! (to 7),
  7 Wonders (to 7), The Resistance: Avalon (5-10), Shadow Hunters (4-8).
- **Lightest teaching load:** Love Letter, Coup, Machi Koro, Kingdomino.

## 5. Honourable mentions

Glass Road (Uwe Rosenberg, 2013) - pick 5 role cards, only 3 resolve, resource conversion.
Fairy Tale (2004) and Paper Tales (2017) - drafting plus tableau.
Shadow Hunters (2005) - hidden-role teams, light, 4-8.
Hoity Toity / Adel verpflichtet (Klaus Teuber, 1990) - simultaneous secret action selection plus
deduction; a genuine ancestor of Citadels' guessing game.
The Resistance: Avalon, Secret Hitler, One Night Ultimate Werewolf - hidden roles only.
Kingdomino, Quadropolis, Fields of Green - building only.
Seasons, Among the Stars, Inis, Blood Rage - drafting with tableau or board.

## 6. Bottom line

- If you want the closest possible feel: **Libertalia / Winds of Galecrest**.
- If you want Citadels plus real city-building depth: **Glory to Rome**, or **Uchronia** on a budget.
- If you want the family-friendly next step: **San Juan** (or **Love Letter** for 15 minutes).
- If you just want more Citadels: buy the **2016 edition / Dark City**.

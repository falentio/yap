# Mascarade — Bluffing Game Guidelines

Mascarade (Bruno Faidutti, Repos Production). 2–13 players, ~20–30 min. A hidden-identity
bluffing game where your own mask is a rumor you are trying to manage.

There are two printings with **different character powers and names**. Everything below is
labeled **[1e]**, **[2e]**, or **[both]**. If you are unsure which box you own: if the King
takes **3** coins it is 1st edition; if the King takes **2** and there is an **Empress** and
**Patron**, it is 2nd edition.

---

## 1. The loop in one paragraph

Everyone starts with 6 coins and one face-down mask. The first four turns are forced
blind swaps, so by the time real play starts nobody knows anything. After that, each turn
you either **look** at your own mask, **swap** it with someone else (or fake the swap), or
**announce** a mask to use its power. Announcements are not declarations of truth — you can
call any mask in play, and if nobody objects you just take the power. The tension is that
anyone may "call" you by claiming the same mask, forcing a reveal where the real holder gets
the power and everyone who was lying pays a fine. First to 13 coins wins.

---

## 2. Setup checklist

- [both] Give every player coins totaling **6**. Wealth stays **public** for the whole game.
- [both] Remaining coins form the **bank**; the **Justice/courthouse board** sits apart from it.
- [both] **4–5 players:** use 6 masks; leftovers go **face up in the middle of the table**
  (they can be swapped with, and announced).
- [both] **6–13 players:** use one mask per player; no middle cards.
- [both] Deal masks **face up**, read every power out loud, let everyone memorize, then flip
  all masks **face down**.
- [both] Youngest player starts; play goes **clockwise**.
- [both] Deck-building constraints for custom sets: **the Judge must be in play**; at least
  **one-third** of the masks must be money-earners (1e: Queen, King, Widow, Fool, Peasants;
  2e: the cards with the coin symbol); **both Peasants or neither**; respect minimum-player
  masks (Peasants and Inquisitor/Guru need **8+**).
- [both] **Opaque table required.** Hands and swaps go under the table; a glass table breaks
  the game.
- Advice from the rulebook: keep your first games to **4–8 players**.

---

## 3. Turn structure

### Turns 1–4 (forced opening)
[both] The first four players, in order, **must** swap. Take your mask and any one other
face-down mask (another player's or a middle card), put both **under the table**, exchange
them **or not**, and return one to each position. You may not look at either card.

### Turn 5 onward — exactly one action
1. **Look at your mask** — secretly view your own card.
2. **Exchange your mask** — take your card and another face-down card, swap-or-fake under
   the table, return one to each position.
3. **Announce your mask** — name a mask in play to use its power.

Hard rules that are commonly misplayed:
- During an exchange, **nobody may look at either card**, and the player whose card was
  taken **may not look at the card returned to them**. It is legal (and funny) to forget
  whether you actually swapped.
- If you **accidentally look** at your mask when it is not your turn, you **must** take the
  Look action on your next turn.
- **The reveal lockout [both]:** if you were **forced to reveal** your mask during the
  *previous* player's turn (you were called, or you called someone, or a Guru/Inquisitor
  pointed at you), you **cannot announce** this turn — you are restricted to exchange/look.
  This is the single most important rule for end-game bluffing (see §6).

---

## 4. Announce & challenge — exact resolution order

1. You announce a mask name, **without showing your card**.
2. Starting with the player on your **left**, going clockwise, every other player in turn may
   **claim** the same mask.
3. **No claimants:** you apply the power **immediately, without revealing**. You never have
   to hold the mask you named — and you do not even need to know what you hold.
4. **One or more claimants:** **all** claimants (including you) reveal their cards.
   - The player who **actually holds** the named mask applies its power immediately — **even
     though it is not their turn**.
   - Every other claimant (including you, if you were bluffing) pays a **1-coin fine** onto
     the **Justice board**.
   - If **nobody** among the claimants holds it, **all** of them pay 1 coin.
5. All cards are flipped face down again; play passes to the next player.

Two rulings that decide games:
- **Judge timing:** fines from a Judge challenge are paid **after** the Judge's power
  resolves, so the Judge does **not** collect the fines generated by that same challenge.
  Same structure for the **Witch/Sorceress**: fines are paid after the fortune-swap resolves.
- **Fines go to the Justice board**, never to the challenger (except the
  Inquisitor/Guru's 4-coin payment, which goes to the player).

---

## 5. Winning and losing

- [both] **13 coins → immediate win.** Check after every single coin movement.
- [both] **Bankruptcy → game ends immediately and the richest player wins.** Ties share the
  win. This makes draining the poorest player a legitimate win condition.
- [both] **Cheat/Cheater: if you hold it and have 10+ coins, you win instantly.** It is a win
  trigger, not a coin gain — and the classic trap is that the player who calls it hands the
  victory to its holder.

---

## 6. Bluffing guidelines (the core skill)

**The mental model:** you are not managing what you hold, you are managing what the table
*believes* about what you hold. Your mask is a claim; the coins are real.

1. **Announce the power you need, not the mask you hold.** Since an unchallenged
   announcement needs no proof, the correct question is never "what am I?" but "what does
   the table currently believe, and what can I get away with?"
2. **Track the information state, not the cards.** Record: who used Look, who exchanged with
   whom, who was revealed and when. Three data points usually beat perfect memory of turn 1.
3. **Exploit the reveal lockout.** A reveal is not just information — it is a **turn of
   freedom** for everyone else. If you reveal during the player to your right's turn, that
   player cannot announce. Conversely, plan reveals so you are not the one locked out when a
   13-coin line opens up.
4. **Challenges are information trades.** Calling reveals **your** card too, for free, to
   everyone. Call only when the expected value is positive — and remember the key asymmetry:
   **if the true holder is a third player, your challenge does not stop the power at all.**
   It only makes the bluffer pay 1 coin. So challenge to punish a bluff and shift tempo, not
   to "cancel" an effect.
5. **Call when you hold the mask.** The cleanest call is being the real holder: you get the
   power *and* the bluffer pays. Watch for the moment someone announces a mask you know is
   sitting in front of you.
6. **Bluff into uncertainty, not into knowledge.** A mask that was just revealed is a bad
   bluff target; a mask lost in the opening four swaps is a great one.
7. **Budget for fines.** Fines are a real cost, and they feed the Justice board, which the
   Judge vacuums. Feeding a Judge you can't control is donating; feeding a Judge who will
   announce it early and often can be a favor you can cash in.
8. **Weaponize the middle cards (4–5 players).** Center masks are public, unowned, and
   swappable — they are the cheapest bluff targets because nobody can hold them.
9. **Stay quiet about your swaps.** Never narrate truthfully. If asked what you swapped,
   smile. Voluntary information is the only thing you can't get back.
10. **Bankruptcy is a loaded gun.** When someone is at 1–2 coins, the Thief, Bishop/Trickster,
    Beggar, Puppeteer, and Witch/Sorceress lines become *game-ending* rather than merely
    profitable. Announce those.

## 7. Character-by-character notes

Powers are quoted from the official rulebooks. "Bluff angle" is strategy, not rules.

| Mask (1e / 2e) | Power | Notes & bluff angle |
|---|---|---|
| **King [1e]** / **Empress [2e]** | Take **3** coins from the bank. | The premium money line. In 1e this is the King; in 2e it is the Empress (the 2e King only takes 2). Pure tempo — good early bluff because everyone wants it. |
| **Queen [1e]** / **King [2e]** | Take **2** coins from the bank. | The bread-and-butter line. In 2e the King is the 2-coin mask. Low-risk, always-available bluff target. |
| **Bishop [1e]** / **Trickster [2e]** | Take **2** coins from the **richest** other player; you choose on a tie. | A real attack, and the cheapest way to drain a leader. Bluff it when you want to look like a threat to the leader. |
| **Judge [both]** | Take **all coins on the Justice board.** | Mandatory in every game. Strongest when the board is loaded; remember the fines from the same challenge are paid **after** it resolves. Bluffing the Judge when the board is fat is a classic. |
| **Fool [both]** | Take **1** coin from the bank, then swap-or-fake **two other players'** masks. | The chaos engine. Great for destroying the table's information without touching your own card. |
| **Witch [1e]** / **Sorceress [2e]** | Swap **your entire fortune** with another player. | The comeback and the leader-killer. Fines against a false Sorceress claim are paid after the swap resolves. Bluff it to scare the leader into a defensive turn. |
| **Widow [both]** | Take coins from the bank until you have **10 total**; no effect if already ≥10. | A ladder, not a sprint — useless at 10+, enormous at 1–2 coins. In 1e the wording is "bring her fortune up to 10"; in 2e it is "exactly 10". |
| **Thief [both]** | Take **1 coin from each neighbor** (left and right). | Low ceiling, but it can bankrupt a 1-coin neighbor and **end the game instantly**. |
| **Spy [both]** | Look at **your own mask and another player's**, then swap-or-fake the two. | The information play: it is a Look *plus* a swap in one action. Best when the table is confused. |
| **Cheat [1e]** / **Cheater [2e]** | If you have **10+ coins, you win immediately.** | Never announce it unless you win — and never call it unless you know the claimant is under 10. |
| **Peasant x2 [both]** | Take **1** coin; if challenged **and both Peasants are revealed**, **each** takes **2**. | Needs 8+ players; both or neither must be in play. The only mask where being *called* is a reward — bluff it hoping for a friendly co-claim. |
| **Inquisitor [1e]** / **Guru [2e]** | Point at a player; they must announce their mask then reveal. If wrong, they **pay you 4 coins**. | Needs **8+ players**. A 4-coin swing, and if they have fewer than 4 coins they pay all they have and **the game ends** (2e rulebook note). |
| **Patron [2e]** | Take **3** from the bank; your two neighbors **each take 1**. | 2e-only. The best "buy goodwill" line — it is the rare power that pays your rivals. |
| **Princess [2e]** | Take **2** from the bank, then choose a player who **reveals their mask to everyone but cannot see it themselves**. | 2e-only. Brutal information denial — the victim never learns their own mask. |
| **Puppeteer [2e]** | Choose two players, take **1 coin from each**; they **physically swap seats** while masks and wealth stay put. | 2e-only. Mangles turn order, neighbors, and everyone's mental map at once. **Caution:** Board Game Arena implements this differently (it moves masks and coins with the players instead of seats) — agree on one reading before you start. |
| **Beggar [2e]** | Going clockwise from your left, each player **richer than you** must give you **1 coin**. | 2e-only. Perfect while poor; the flip side is it broadcasts exactly how poor you are. |

## 8. Common mistakes and edge-case rulings

- You do **not** have to hold the mask you announce, and you do **not** have to know what you hold.
- You do **not** reveal on an unchallenged announcement. Cards stay face down unless a
  challenge, the Guru/Inquisitor, or the Princess forces a reveal.
- **Nobody holding the announced mask** still means every claimant pays 1 coin.
- The power of the true holder fires **out of turn**.
- Fines always land on the **Justice board** (except the Inquisitor/Guru's 4 coins, paid to
  the player).
- Judge does **not** collect fines generated by its own challenge; same for Witch/Sorceress.
- Look-by-accident forces you to take Look on your turn.
- Forced reveal on the immediately preceding turn blocks your Announce this turn.
- **Bankruptcy ends the game at once**, richest wins, ties share.
- Cheat/Cheater is a **win condition at 10+**, not a coin gain.
- Peasant bonus requires **both Peasants actually revealed** in the same challenge.
- Exchange rules: no peeking by anyone, and the victim may not peek at the returned card.
- 2-player and 3-player games use special variants (each player holds multiple cards, plus a
  "protected" card in the 2-player game) — the 2-player game is explicitly labeled "for even
  more experienced players."

---

## 9. Table etiquette

- Keep your coin stack visibly countable at all times.
- Keep turns fast; hesitation is a tell and the game sags at 8+ players.
- Use an opaque table and keep swap hands fully hidden.
- Do not narrate your swaps truthfully, and do not "helpfully" remind people of what they did.

### Known discrepancies to settle at the table
- **Puppeteer:** the printed 2e rulebook says the two chosen players **switch seats** and their
  masks and coins **stay at the original seats**. The Board Game Arena implementation instead
  moves the masks and coins along with the players. Pick one before play.
- **King's value flips between editions** (3 in 1e, 2 in 2e). The "3-coin" mask in 2e is the
  **Empress**. Confirm which set your scenario sheet lists before calling "I'm the King."
- **Widow wording:** 1e says "bring her fortune up to 10"; 2e says "until you have exactly 10
  total." Functionally the same, but read the card you own.
